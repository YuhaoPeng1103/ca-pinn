\begin{abstract}
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) have emerged as a promising framework for solving partial differential equations by embedding physical laws into neural network training. However, standard PINNs suffer from well-documented training pathologies: multi-task gradient imbalance among PDE, data, and boundary condition losses, and violation of temporal causality in time-dependent problems. Through full-convergence experiments (30,000 epochs) on the 2D shallow water equations with a localized drainage sink, complemented by generalization studies on the Burgers equation and the 2D Navier--Stokes cylinder wake, we make four empirical contributions. First, ReLoBRaLo gradient-based loss balancing with \textit{fixed-weight parameter priors} recovers Manning's $n$, drainage coefficient $C$, and inflow $q_{x0}$ with sub-percent accuracy ($n$: 0.045\%, $C$: 0.047\%), while all tested methods attain comparable solution-field accuracy. Second, we discover that balancing the prior terms re-ill-poses the inverse problem: prior weights decay toward zero and parameters drift ($C$ error degrading from 0.047\% to 37.2\% at full convergence), yielding the design principle that \textit{adaptive balancing must apply only to conflicting multi-task losses, never to parameter priors}. Third, causal time-domain weighting degrades performance on \textit{all three} benchmark problems under Adam-only optimization---including strongly time-dependent vortex-shedding dynamics---both alone and in combination with ReLoBRaLo, indicating it does not robustly transfer without second-order fine-tuning. Fourth, sparse-observation experiments show that parameter recovery degrades under limited data when the modules are combined. These findings provide concrete, reproducible guidance for PINN-based hydrodynamic inverse problems.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item ReLoBRaLo loss balancing with fixed-weight parameter priors recovers Manning's $n$, drainage coefficient $C$, and inflow discharge with sub-percent accuracy on a 2D shallow-water inverse problem.
\item Adaptive balancing must apply only to conflicting multi-task losses, never to parameter priors, to avoid re-ill-posing inverse problems.
\item Causal time-domain weighting fails to transfer to Adam-only optimization across three PDE benchmarks, including strongly time-dependent vortex shedding.
\item Balancing parameter priors re-ill-poses the inverse problem; fixed priors keep parameter recovery sub-percent.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
% ======================================================================

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019physics} integrate governing physical equations as soft constraints during neural network training via automatic differentiation. This paradigm has achieved remarkable success across fluid mechanics \cite{raissi2020hidden}, solid mechanics, and heat transfer, offering mesh-free solutions and natural handling of inverse problems where physical parameters are inferred from sparse observations.

Despite their conceptual elegance, PINNs face well-documented training pathologies that limit their reliability, particularly for nonlinear time-dependent problems. We identify two fundamental issues:

\textbf{(1) Causality violation.} Standard PINN training simultaneously minimizes PDE residuals across the entire spatio-temporal domain. For time-evolution equations, this means the network attempts to satisfy the PDE at late times before it has learned the early-time dynamics---a fundamental violation of the causal structure inherent in initial value problems \cite{wang2022respecting}.

\textbf{(2) Multi-task gradient imbalance.} PINN training involves minimizing a composite loss with terms for PDE residuals, data fitting, boundary conditions, and initial conditions. These terms have different scales, converge at different rates, and compete for gradient direction \cite{wang2021understanding}. Fixed loss weights, as used in most PINN implementations, lead to one term dominating training at the expense of others.

While prior work has addressed each issue in isolation, systematic full-convergence comparisons remain scarce, and interactions such as how loss balancing affects inverse-problem identifiability are under-explored. Most PINN papers report results at short training budgets, leaving open which methods actually help at convergence.

\textbf{Contributions.} We evaluate two modular PINN training strategies---ReLoBRaLo loss balancing and causal time-domain weighting---at full convergence (30,000 epochs) on the 2D shallow water equations with a localized drainage sink, a nonlinear hyperbolic inverse problem of practical hydraulic importance. Our experiments yield a key design principle: adaptive loss balancing must exclude parameter priors, which retain fixed weights to preserve identifiability. Across three PDE benchmarks (shallow water, Burgers, Navier--Stokes), we show that causal weighting fails to transfer to Adam-only optimization and interacts negatively with gradient-based balancing, while loss balancing preserves parameter recovery in inverse problems but does not help strongly nonlinear forward dynamics.

\section{Related Work}
% ======================================================================

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Networks}

Raissi et al. \cite{raissi2019physics} introduced PINNs, demonstrating solutions to both forward problems (Burgers, Schr\"{o}dinger, Allen-Cahn) and inverse problems (Navier-Stokes, KdV) using fully-connected networks with tanh activations and two-stage Adam+L-BFGS optimization. Subsequent work extended PINNs to diverse applications in fluid dynamics \cite{cai2021physics}, though training difficulties for stiff or multi-scale problems were quickly recognized.

\subsection{Fixing Gradient Pathologies}

Wang et al. \cite{wang2021understanding, wang2022improved} provided theoretical analysis of PINN training pathologies through the lens of neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory, showing that gradient magnitudes of different loss terms can differ by orders of magnitude. They proposed learning rate annealing for loss weights. Bischof and Kraus \cite{bischof2021multi} proposed ReLoBRaLo (Relative Loss Balancing with Random Lookback), which balances losses adaptively using exponentially-smoothed relative gradient norms with log-normal exploration noise. Other approaches include PCGrad \cite{yu2020gradient} for gradient conflict resolution and GradNorm \cite{chen2018gradnorm} for task-balancing in multi-task learning.

\subsection{Causal and Curriculum Training}

Krishnapriyan et al. \cite{krishnapriyan2021characterizing} showed that PINNs fail on simple convection equations when the time domain is too large. Wang et al. \cite{wang2022respecting} formalized this as a causality violation and proposed weighting PDE residuals exponentially by the accumulated error in preceding time windows, enabling PINNs to solve previously intractable long-time integration problems.

\subsection{Shallow Water Equations}

The 2D shallow water equations are a fundamental model in coastal engineering, flood routing, and river hydraulics, forming a nonlinear hyperbolic system of conservation laws. Prior PINN work on SWE is limited: most studies use 1D simplifications \cite{he2023physics} or steady-state assumptions. The full 2D SWE with friction, a localized drainage sink, and unknown parameters presents a challenging multi-scale inverse test case combining a smooth base flow with a localized high-frequency depression.

\section{Methodology}
% ======================================================================

We consider physics-informed neural networks for solving partial differential equations of the general form:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{N}[\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, t); \boldsymbol{\lambda}] = 0, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega, t \in [0, T]
    \label{eq:general_pde}
\end{equation}

subject to initial condition $\mathcal{I}[\mathbf{u}] = 0$ and boundary condition $\mathcal{B}[\mathbf{u}] = 0$, where $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ denotes unknown physical parameters (inverse problem). A neural network $\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ with parameters $\theta$ approximates the solution. The standard PINN loss is:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{N_f}\sum_{i=1}^{N_f} \|\mathcal{N}[\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_f^i, t_f^i)]\|^2}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}}
    + \lambda_d \underbrace{\frac{1}{N_d}\sum_{i=1}^{N_d} \|\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_d^i, t_d^i) - \mathbf{u}_d^i\|^2}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}}
    + \lambda_b \underbrace{\frac{1}{N_b}\sum_{i=1}^{N_b} \|\mathcal{B}[\mathbf{u}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_b^i, t_b^i)]\|^2}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{BC}}}
    \label{eq:vanilla_loss}
\end{equation}

where $\{\mathbf{x}_f^i, t_f^i\}_{i=1}^{N_f}$ are collocation points, $\{\mathbf{x}_d^i, t_d^i, \mathbf{u}_d^i\}_{i=1}^{N_d}$ are observations, and $\lambda_d$, $\lambda_b$ are fixed weights. We identify three limitations of this formulation and propose three orthogonal remedies.

\subsection{Causal Time-Domain Weighting}

The PDE loss in Eq.~\ref{eq:vanilla_loss} treats all time instants equally. For time-dependent problems, this violates the causal principle: the solution at time $t$ depends only on earlier times. We remedy this by partitioning the time domain into $K$ chunks $[t_0, t_1], [t_1, t_2], \dots, [t_{K-1}, t_K]$ and weighting the PDE loss in each chunk by an exponential gate:

\begin{equation}
    w_k = \exp\left(-\varepsilon \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}^{(j)}\right), \quad k = 1, \dots, K
    \label{eq:causal_weight}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}^{(j)}$ is the mean PDE residual in chunk $j$, and $\varepsilon$ controls the strictness of the causal gate. When $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}^{(j)}$ is large (early chunks are under-resolved), $w_k$ for later chunks is exponentially suppressed. As early chunks are learned, the gate opens progressively to later times.

The causally-weighted PDE loss becomes:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}^{\text{causal}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}^{(k)}
    \label{eq:causal_pde_loss}
\end{equation}

This enforces a temporal curriculum: the network must first solve the early-time dynamics before being exposed to the full evolution. For the SWE system, we use $K=72$ chunks of 50 seconds each over $T=3600$s, with $\varepsilon=0.1$.

\subsection{ReLoBRaLo Gradient-Based Loss Balancing}

Fixed weights $\lambda_d, \lambda_b$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:vanilla_loss} cannot adapt to the evolving gradient landscape during training. We adopt ReLoBRaLo \cite{bischof2021multi}, which updates loss weights at each iteration based on gradient statistics:

\begin{equation}
    \hat{\lambda}_k^{(t)} = \alpha \hat{\lambda}_k^{(t-1)} + (1-\alpha) \frac{\|\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_k\|}{\max_j \|\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_j\|}
    \label{eq:relobralo_ema}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \lambda_k^{(t)} = \frac{K \cdot \hat{\lambda}_k^{(t)}}{\sum_j \hat{\lambda}_j^{(t)}} \cdot \exp(\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2))
    \label{eq:relobralo_final}
\end{equation}

where $\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_k$ is the gradient of the $k$-th loss term with respect to the last hidden layer weights, $\alpha = 0.9$ controls the exponential moving average, and $\sigma^2 = 0.01$ adds log-normal exploration noise. The gradient is computed on the last layer only for computational efficiency while still capturing relative gradient scale information.

We balance $K=3$ conflicting multi-task losses: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PDE}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BC}}$. The parameter prior $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}$ (inverse problems) is \textit{excluded} from balancing and keeps a fixed weight $\lambda_{\text{prior}} = 1$. The balanced total loss is:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{k=1}^{3} \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_k \;+\; \lambda_{\text{prior}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}

\subsection{A Gradient-Decoupling Argument: Why Balancing Priors Fails}

Our experiments (Table~\ref{tab:balprior}) show that including $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}}$ among the balanced losses re-ill-poses the inverse problem: its weight decays to zero and the physical parameters drift. This failure has a simple, provable mechanism, which we formalize as follows.

\textbf{Proposition (gradient decoupling).} Let the balancing anchor $\phi$ be a parameter subset with respect to which gradient norms are estimated (here, the last-layer weights $W_L$). If a loss term $\mathcal{L}_k$ is a function only of a parameter subset $\theta_P$ disjoint from $\phi$, i.e., $\partial \phi / \partial \theta_P = 0$ and $\mathcal{L}_k = \mathcal{L}_k(\theta_P)$, then $\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_k \equiv 0$ for all training iterations. Consequently, the ReLoBRaLo update (Eq.~\ref{eq:relobralo_ema}) drives $\hat{\lambda}_k^{(t)} \to 0$ geometrically at rate $\alpha$.

\textit{Proof.} By the chain rule, $\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_k = \left(\partial \mathcal{L}_k / \partial \theta_P\right) \left(\partial \theta_P / \partial \phi\right) = \left(\partial \mathcal{L}_k / \partial \theta_P\right) \cdot 0 = 0$, since $\theta_P$ and $\phi$ are independent variables. Substituting $\|\nabla_\phi \mathcal{L}_k\| = 0$ into Eq.~\ref{eq:relobralo_ema} yields $\hat{\lambda}_k^{(t)} = \alpha^t \hat{\lambda}_k^{(0)} \to 0$. $\blacksquare$

In our SWE inverse problem, the prior $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}} = 10(n - 0.03)^2 + 5(C - 0.05)^2 + 5(q_{x0} - 1.0)^2$ depends only on the three scalar physical parameters $\theta_P = \{n_{\text{raw}}, C_{\text{raw}}, q_{x0,\text{raw}}\}$, which do not influence the network outputs through $W_L$. The proposition therefore predicts $\lambda_{\text{prior}} \to 0$---exactly the observed behavior (Table~\ref{tab:balprior}).

\textbf{Remedy.} The failure is a property of the \textit{anchor choice}, not of balancing itself. Two fixes follow directly: (i) exclude anchor-decoupled losses from balancing (our design principle, which we validate empirically); or (ii) estimate gradient norms over the \textit{full} parameter vector $\theta$ instead of the last layer, in which case $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}} \neq 0$ and the balancer can assign the prior a meaningful weight. Option (i) is simpler and avoids the computational cost of full-parameter gradient norms; we adopt it throughout. The same argument applies to any gradient-norm-based balancer (e.g., GradNorm \cite{chen2018gradnorm}) and to any decoupled auxiliary loss, so the principle generalizes beyond inverse-problem priors.

\subsection{Fourier Feature Encoding}

Standard MLPs with tanh activation exhibit spectral bias---they preferentially learn low-frequency components of the solution \cite{rahaman2019spectral, tancik2020fourier}. The SWE drainage problem contains a localized depression at $(x,y)=(50,50)$ m with characteristic width $\sim 12$ m, producing high spatial frequencies. We prepend a random Fourier feature (RFF) mapping \cite{tancik2020fourier} to the input layer:

\begin{equation}
    \gamma(\mathbf{v}) = \bigl[\cos(2\pi \mathbf{B} \mathbf{v}),\; \sin(2\pi \mathbf{B} \mathbf{v})\bigr]
    \label{eq:rff}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{v} = [x/L_x, y/L_y, t/T] \in [0,1]^3$ and $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times 3}$ is a fixed random matrix with entries $B_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. We use $m=64$ (producing $128$ Fourier features) and $\sigma=1.0$. The Fourier features are passed to a standard MLP with 4 hidden layers of 128 neurons each.

\subsection{2D Shallow Water Equations}

We apply the proposed balancing strategy to the 2D shallow water equations in conservation form with discharges $(q_x, q_y) = (hu, hv)$ as primary variables:

\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial h}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial q_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial q_y}{\partial y} + S_d &= 0 \label{eq:swe_cont} \\
    \frac{\partial q_x}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\frac{q_x^2}{h}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\frac{q_y q_x}{h}\right) + gh\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial x} - S_{0x}\right) + \frac{g n^2 q_x \sqrt{q_x^2+q_y^2}}{h^{7/3}} &= 0 \label{eq:swe_xmom} \\
    \frac{\partial q_y}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\frac{q_y^2}{h}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\frac{q_y q_x}{h}\right) + gh\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial y} - S_{0y}\right) + \frac{g n^2 q_y \sqrt{q_x^2+q_y^2}}{h^{7/3}} &= 0 \label{eq:swe_ymom}
\end{align}

where $h$ is water depth, $g=9.81$ m/s$^2$, $S_{0x}=0.001$ and $S_{0y}=0$ are bed slopes, $n$ is Manning's roughness coefficient, and $S_d = C \cdot 0.5 \cdot \exp(-d/12) \cdot h$ is a localized drainage sink term centered at $(50,50)$ m with spatial scale 12 m. The domain is $\Omega = [0,100] \times [0,100]$ m$^2$, $T = 3600$ s.

Boundary conditions are: upstream inflow $q_x = q_{x0}$, $q_y = 0$ at $x=0$; free outflow $\partial h/\partial x = 0$ at $x=100$; free-slip at $y=0,100$. We solve the inverse problem: given $N_d = 800$ noisy water depth observations ($\pm 0.5\%$ Gaussian noise), jointly infer the solution fields $(h, q_x, q_y)$ and the three unknown parameters: Manning's $n$ (true value $0.03$), drainage coefficient $C$ (true $0.05$), and inflow $q_{x0}$ (true $1.0$ m$^2$/s).

Parameter priors are incorporated as soft constraints: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prior}} = 10(n-0.03)^2 + 5(C-0.05)^2 + 5(q_{x0}-1.0)^2$. The network outputs $h$ through a softplus activation to enforce positivity.

\subsection{Reference Solution and Verification}
\label{sec:reference}

The reference solution is computed with a first-order Lax--Friedrichs finite-volume scheme on a cell-centered grid, using the same governing equations, drainage sink, and boundary conditions as the PINN residual. The solver is explicit (CFL number $0.5$) with ghost-cell boundary conditions: specified inflow discharge at $x=0$, free outflow at $x=100$, and free-slip walls at $y=0,100$. Two checks verify the reference. First, in the absence of drainage ($C=0$) the solution remains at the Manning normal depth $h=(n q_{x0}/\sqrt{S_{0x}})^{3/5}$ to machine precision. Second, the scheme is mass-conserving: at steady state the downstream outflow plus the integrated drainage sink equals the upstream inflow to within $0.1\%$, and this imbalance shrinks as the grid is refined. Reference fields are computed on a $200\times200$ grid, and grid convergence of the $L_2$ field error was confirmed across $80\times80$, $160\times160$, and $200\times200$ grids.

\section{Experiments}
% ======================================================================

We conduct four sets of experiments: (1) main comparison of the ReLoBRaLo-balanced configuration against a vanilla PINN baseline on the SWE inverse problem; (2) an ablation study over the causal and ReLoBRaLo modules ($2^2 = 4$ combinations); (3) generalization to the Burgers equation and the 2D Navier--Stokes cylinder wake; and (4) a sparse-observation robustness study.

\textbf{Implementation.} All experiments use the same network architecture: Fourier feature encoding ($m=64 \rightarrow 128$ features) + 4-hidden-layer MLP with 128 neurons per layer and tanh activation. Training uses Adam optimizer with initial learning rate $\eta=3\times10^{-4}$, ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler (patience $1000$, factor $0.5$), and gradient clipping at $\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\max}=1.0$. For the main SWE experiments, we train for $30,000$ epochs with $N_f = 8,000$ collocation points. All experiments run on a single CPU. Each training run takes approximately 2--4 hours on a modern laptop CPU.

\textbf{Design principle discovered.} Our experiments reveal a critical interaction between loss balancing and inverse problems: when ReLoBRaLo is applied to \textit{all} loss terms including parameter priors, the prior weights are driven to near zero ($\lambda_{\text{prior}} \to 0$), allowing the physical parameters to drift toward solutions that minimize PDE and data losses but violate prior knowledge (Table~\ref{tab:balprior}: $\epsilon_C$ degrades from 0.047\% to 37.2\% at full convergence). Excluding the prior term from balancing and keeping its weight fixed preserves sub-percent parameter recovery. This yields a concrete design principle: \textbf{adaptive loss balancing should be applied only to conflicting multi-task losses (PDE/data/BC), while parameter priors in inverse problems must retain fixed weights}.

\textbf{Causal training on quasi-steady problems.} Causal time-domain weighting, whether alone ($\epsilon_h=9.22\times10^{-3}$) or combined with ReLoBRaLo ($\epsilon_h=1.36\times10^{-2}$), degrades performance on the SWE problem. This is because the SWE solution is \textit{quasi-steady}: the temporal variation is a 5\% sinusoidal perturbation of a steady base flow, so temporal causality is a weak prior that conflicts with the dominant steady-state structure. As the generalization experiments below (Burgers, Navier--Stokes) show, this failure is not specific to quasi-steady dynamics: causal weighting also degrades strongly time-dependent vortex shedding under Adam-only optimization, indicating that it does not robustly transfer without the L-BFGS fine-tuning stage used in the original causal-PINN work \cite{wang2022respecting}.

\textbf{Key findings:} (1) ReLoBRaLo with fixed-weight priors preserves sub-percent parameter recovery ($\epsilon_n$: 0.045\%, $\epsilon_C$: 0.047\%), while all configurations attain comparable field accuracy ($\epsilon_h \approx 0.5$--$1.4\%$). (3) The combination CR performs worst, indicating that when the causal gate perturbs the PDE loss statistics, the gradient-based balancer amplifies the perturbation rather than compensating for it---a negative interaction we did not anticipate. This run predates our prior-fixing principle and balanced all four losses including priors, so its ReLoBRaLo result conflates two failure modes---incomplete EMA stabilization and prior-weight decay---whereas the full-scale ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) isolates the prior-fixing effect. Practical guideline: at short budgets vanilla is competitive; the ReLoBRaLo advantage emerges only at full convergence with fixed priors.

% ======================================================================
\section{Discussion}
% ======================================================================

\textbf{When modules help and when they hurt.} Our results refine the common assumption that causal training and loss balancing are universally beneficial for PINNs. Loss balancing (ReLoBRaLo) with fixed priors preserves sub-percent parameter recovery on the SWE inverse problem and improves field accuracy on Burgers (3.2\%), but does \textit{not} help the strongly nonlinear Navier--Stokes cylinder wake, where vanilla training already attains the lowest error. Causal training degrades performance on \textit{all three} problem classes under Adam-only optimization, including the strongly time-dependent Burgers and vortex-shedding dynamics where it was hypothesized to help most. Since the causal-PINN literature pairs causal weighting with L-BFGS fine-tuning \cite{wang2022respecting}, we hypothesize that causal gating interacts with the optimizer choice, and we caution practitioners against adopting causal weighting in Adam-only pipelines without validation. The negative interaction between causal weighting and ReLoBRaLo reproduces across all three benchmarks, suggesting it is a robust phenomenon rather than a problem-specific artifact.

\textbf{Design principle for inverse problems.} We distill one actionable guideline: \textit{Balance conflicting losses, never priors.} Parameter priors encode identifiable physical constants; letting their weights decay toward zero reintroduces ill-posedness---at full convergence, balancing the prior drives $\epsilon_C$ from 0.047\% to 37.2\% (Table~\ref{tab:balprior}), and the fixed-prior configuration also recovers parameters far more accurately than learning-rate annealing \cite{wang2021understanding} ($\epsilon_n$: 0.045\% vs.\ 3.06\%).

\textbf{Limitations.} (1) Our SWE test case uses synthetic data with a known analytical drainage-sink form; real-world bathymetry and roughness would add complexity. (2) The fixed random Fourier projection could be improved by adaptive frequency tuning \cite{wang2022improved}. (3) Automatic module selection remains open, since causal weighting and ReLoBRaLo interact negatively.

\textbf{Broader impact.} The fixed-prior balancing strategy is a drop-in PINN training improvement with modest overhead (38\% for ReLoBRaLo), valuable for problems with localized features (drainage, shocks, boundary layers) and multi-physics couplings.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ======================================================================

We conducted full-convergence experiments (30,000 epochs) on a 2D shallow-water inverse problem with a localized drainage sink, plus generalization studies on the Burgers equation and the 2D Navier--Stokes cylinder wake, and distilled an actionable design principle for robust PINN training. Our contributions are: (1) ReLoBRaLo loss balancing with \textit{fixed-weight parameter priors} recovers Manning's $n$, drainage coefficient $C$, and inflow $q_{x0}$ with sub-percent accuracy; (2) a design principle that adaptive balancing must exclude parameter priors to avoid re-ill-posing inverse problems, backed by a gradient-decoupling argument; (3) a conditional view of training modules across three PDE benchmarks---causal weighting fails to transfer to Adam-only optimization, while loss balancing preserves parameter recovery in inverse problems but does not help strongly nonlinear forward dynamics.
